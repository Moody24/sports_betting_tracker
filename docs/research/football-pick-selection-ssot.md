# Football pick-selection SSOT

Version: **1.0**

Effective: **2026-09-10, America/New_York**

Canonical file: **docs/research/football-pick-selection-ssot.md**

This is the standing method for researching football picks and constructing plays or parlays. Each session changes the evidence and selections while retaining this decision process.

**Core rule: establish the current offense before evaluating its individual props. Every material roster or coaching change must reach the opportunity assessment and the affected pick decisions. Mentioning a player in a roster table is insufficient.**

This standard governs manual NFL research using PropFinder. Team sides/totals, when requested, use the same team assessment with the appropriate outcome analysis. Other sports need an explicit mapping of opportunities and markets; they do not justify silently replacing the method. Automated model development remains governed by the project's architecture and validation documents.

The only editable method lives here. Agent instructions and the shared brain link to it. Session worksheets, exports, historical snapshots and postmortems are evidence records, not competing standards.

**The fixed sequence**

Brief → current roster → offensive opportunity → defensive matchup → comparable evidence → market and price comparison → card construction → publication checks → postmortem.

New information reopens the affected steps. It does not require inventing a new method or repeating unrelated research.

**1. Establish the brief and the evidence cutoff**

Record the game, date, venue, scheduled kickoff, current ET time, pregame/live status, sportsbook, allowed markets, requested number of legs, and any explicit touchdown or other constraint.

Carry forward standing preferences; distinguish them from conditions attached to an earlier ticket. Use PropFinder as the primary market and game-log research source. Use the user's current sportsbook preference and identify that book on every quoted selection.

Default to offensive player props. Include kicker markets when they are part of the requested non-defensive scope. Exclude defensive player props unless the user explicitly changes that scope. Defensive personnel and scheme remain required matchup research.

Set an information cutoff. After kickoff, distinguish live analysis from a retrospective pregame assessment. Preserve earlier prices as historical quotes. Do not present a post-kickoff correction as a newly available pregame bet.

**2. Reconcile the entire current roster and coaching staff**

Start with official transactions, current roster/depth chart, injury reports and availability news. Verify actual gameday inactives when published. Use observed recent usage and credible coaching statements to interpret roles. A depth chart establishes listed order; it does not establish snaps, routes, touches or a shadow-coverage assignment.

Include players who have no available prop. Offensive linemen, reserve backs and rotational receivers can change other players' opportunities.

Complete a change register for both teams:

| Change | Prior context → current context | Offensive opportunity affected | Props affected | Evidence/time | Uncertainty or next trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrivals, departures, returns and rookies | Player, team, role and availability | Routes, targets, carries, protection, scoring work or pace | Every affected player/market | Primary source where possible | Verified, bounded assumption or unresolved |
| Health and workload | Healthy, limited, returning, absent | Participation and task changes | Direct and downstream markets | Practice report plus role evidence | No invented snap cap |
| Coaches and schemes | Caller, formation, blocking or coverage changes | Play mix and opportunity distribution | Team and player markets | Current reporting/usage | Separate intended from observed usage |

Explicitly account for the starting QB, primary and rotational RBs, all plausible route earners at WR/TE/FB, offensive line changes, play caller, and key opposing defenders.

For every meaningful arrival or return, account for departures and unavailable teammates as well. A new receiver can absorb vacated work, change formations, attract coverage or sustain more drives. Do not assume every target comes directly from the same incumbent.

**Completion rule:** each material change has a documented impact or a supported reason for no material impact. An unresolved change stays visible in the affected candidates.

**3. Build one shared offensive opportunity assessment per team**

Evaluate ability, opportunity and expected efficiency separately. Established talent does not guarantee volume; limited history does not erase a clearly supported new role.

Map the likely offense before selecting props:

| Component | Required assessment |
|---|---|
| Team volume | Plausible plays, dropbacks, designed runs and pace under ordinary game scripts |
| Protection | Offensive line continuity/rotation; pressure responses; who releases into routes or stays to block |
| Passing distribution | Expected route participation, alignment, target priority, depth of target and receiver competition |
| Rushing distribution | Starter/rotation, designed QB runs, scrambles, short-yardage work and possible kneels |
| Scoring opportunities | Who receives inside-the-20/10/5 work where supported; pass/run choices and competing finishers |
| Personnel groups | Two/three-WR sets, multiple TEs, motion, personnel substitutions and their effect on routes |

Use measured estimates when supported. Otherwise use clearly labeled, justified scenarios or qualitative bounds. Do not invent precise projections to make six selections fit.

Reconcile the whole team: proposed targets must fit the passing volume with room for other receivers and unallocated throws; carries must fit the rushing volume and rotation. Route, target and touchdown shares must use clearly defined denominators. Position-group allowances are not an individual player's forecast.

Evaluate at least a competitive game, a lead and a deficit. Record how the opportunity distribution changes. Selected legs need not win under every script, but their common assumptions and failure paths must be visible.

**The roster-impact check:** would the candidate assessment change if the new player, returning starter or altered rotation were removed? If the answer should be yes but the written analysis is unchanged, the roster update has not been applied.

**4. Match that offense against the current defense**

Analyze defensive vulnerabilities through the offensive role that can exploit them.

| Matchup area | Connect the evidence to the prop |
|---|---|
| Pass rush versus protection | Time to throw, checkdowns, blocking assignments, scrambles and failed drives |
| Coverage versus deployment | Actual or expected outside/slot alignment, route depth, zone/man concepts, safety help and motion |
| Run defense versus rushing design | Front health, box structure, blocking scheme, interior/edge fit and workload |
| Red-zone defense | Scoring opportunities and likely finishers; distinguish drive creation from touchdown conversion |
| Environment | Current weather, surface, venue, travel/rest and pace; identify supported effects and uncertainty |

State the mechanism and the strongest competing effect. Pressure can increase checkdowns and also eliminate routes. An outside threat can divert targets and also open space. Improved offense can produce more opportunities for several players.

Label coverage assignments and tactical effects as inferences unless verified. Reassess last season's opponent numbers after defensive personnel or coordinator changes. Opponent rank, a single head-to-head game or a coach's praise cannot establish an edge by itself.

**5. Build comparable historical evidence consistently**

Use PropFinder logs and filters to test the current-role hypothesis. Keep the full sample visible alongside relevant subsets.

Record season/date range, regular season/playoffs, sample size, injuries or early exits, team/QB, meaningful teammate availability and role changes. Report what a filter controls for and what it leaves unresolved.

- Examine both longer-run ability and recent deployment. Explain meaningful differences.
- Compare similar QB, teammate and role conditions where available. Verify shared game dates rather than assuming all appearances overlapped.
- Treat old-team, rookie, small-sample and injury-affected histories as limited evidence. Support a new role with current deployment, vacated opportunity and appropriate comparisons; do not automatically eliminate newcomers.
- Apply the same uncertainty rule to incumbents. A QB-matched sample does not control for a rebuilt receiving room.
- Do not select filters because they maximize a desired hit rate. Retain contrary games and explain exclusions.
- Separate actual zeros, non-participation, missing records and potential sportsbook voids. Preserve raw data and state any adjusted sample.
- Historical hit rates, PF ratings and a plausible box score are not calibrated win probabilities.
- Resolve material conflicts in names, teams, periods, units and metrics before relying on them. Record unresolved conflicts instead of choosing the favorable value.

**Completion rule:** the evidence explains why the historical opportunity is transferable, partly transferable or not transferable. The same reasoning applies to every candidate.

**6. Sweep the market and compare the actual alternatives**

Inventory all reasonable available markets for the permitted players. Cover both directions, main and useful alternate lines, passing, attempts/completions, QB interceptions, rushing/carries, receiving/receptions, combined yards, touchdowns, longest plays and period markets where offered. Include the permitted kicker markets.

Record board filters, export time, player/market coverage and unavailable or inaccessible categories. A row count does not mean every row received the same depth of analysis. Broad screening may reject a market family for a documented role or data reason; shortlisted selections require a complete pick record.

For each serious candidate, compare:

1. The opposite direction at the exact offered line.
2. The same player's other suitable markets.
3. Useful lower/higher alternatives and their prices.
4. Another player's opportunity in the same offense.
5. Passing on the bet.

Use the same criteria throughout:

| Criterion | Required question |
|---|---|
| Role | What current evidence supports the opportunities needed? |
| Team allocation | Have all material arrivals, departures and returns been applied? |
| Matchup | What concrete mechanism helps, and what could counter it? |
| Sample | What is comparable, and which differences remain? |
| Threshold | What workload and efficiency are needed to clear this exact line? |
| Price | Which book offered it, when, and what tradeoff do alternatives require? |
| Failure | What ordinary event or script makes this lose? |
| Selection | Why prefer this market to the actual alternatives? |

A lower threshold provides statistical margin at a price. It is not automatically better value. A touchdown, longest-play or short-period market can qualify, but requires evidence appropriate to that event; generic team strength is insufficient.

Verify player, market, direction, period, threshold, book and observation time. PropFinder's headline may show another book's best price. Opposite sides can have different thresholds. Distinguish an identified-book aggregator quote from direct sportsbook confirmation.

Use stable decision labels:

- **Candidate:** substantive case exists; comparison or checks remain.
- **Conditional:** a named material assumption or availability/price check remains; state the trigger.
- **Selected:** the comparison is complete, the opportunity is supported, uncertainties are stated and the exact quote is recorded. This means chosen for the requested card, not proven positive value.
- **Pass:** rejected at the offered conditions; record the reason. Passing an over does not endorse an under.
- **Unavailable:** no usable quote, inaccessible evidence or closed market; do not invent an offer.

Keep value assessment separate: **quantified** only with a justified probability estimate and method; otherwise **unquantified**. Do not fabricate confidence percentages, price targets or weighted scores.

For a no-push single with decimal return d, break-even probability is 1/d; expected net return per unit is p × d − 1. The unknown is the justified p. Account for vig and market settlement when comparing prices. These identities do not supply that probability.

**7. Construct the card from eligible picks**

Select legs after the individual comparisons. Do not begin with a desired payout or a story and search only for supporting props.

Reconcile the card against the shared offensive assessment. Check target/carry competition, shared scoring dependence, overlapping exposure to one player and common failure scenarios. Correlation can help or hurt the ticket and affect its offered price; it is not automatically desirable or disqualifying.

A plausible all-win box score is a consistency check only. Never multiply historical hit rates or single-leg prices and label the result a same-game-parlay probability or sportsbook quote.

Obtain the actual combined quote before describing a card as priced and ready. Without it, label the card provisional and do not claim a payout or positive expected value.

Respect the requested leg count, but do not lower the evidence standard to fill it. If fewer legs qualify, identify the shortfall and show any additional candidates as conditional with their unresolved requirements. Do not disguise a forced sixth leg as equally supported.

**8. Freeze, recheck and present**

Before finalizing, recheck material roster/injury news, gameday inactives when available, weather, relevant market moves, and the exact lines/book. A changed input triggers reassessment of all affected props using the same method.

Preserve the original decision record with ET timestamp and method version. Later revisions receive a new timestamp and explicit before/after reason; placed tickets and earlier recommendations remain intact.

The user-facing delivery contains:

- The meaningful roster changes and their offensive consequences.
- The selected or provisional card with exact market, book, price and quote status.
- Each pick's current role, matchup mechanism, evidence limitations and strongest failure case.
- The relevant alternatives passed over and why; attach the broader sweep when lengthy.
- Shared parlay risks, unresolved conditions and the information cutoff.

Do not claim full coverage, final availability, sportsbook acceptance, a calibrated probability or positive value unless verified.

**9. Audit outcomes without rewriting the pregame case**

Retrieve the exact final recommendation and any actual placed ticket separately. Verify results from official box scores/gamebooks; verify sportsbook grading independently where relevant.

For every leg compare expected versus observed participation, opportunities, efficiency and outcome. Record whether the stated mechanism occurred. Distinguish a statistical clear from settlement and a good result from well-supported reasoning.

Classify the finding:

| Finding | Response within this method |
|---|---|
| Missing fact | Correct the roster/source record and affected decisions |
| Missing application | Propagate the known change into offensive allocation and pick comparison |
| Unsupported inference | Correct the role-to-matchup or opportunity-to-event argument |
| Pricing/quote error | Correct book, threshold, period, price or settlement assumptions |
| Probability error | Correct the model/calibration claim; retain descriptive evidence |
| Unexpected event or ordinary variance | Record it; do not convert the outcome into a new universal rule |

One win or loss does not establish an edge. Review probability calibration, prices/closing comparisons and realized results over comparable samples when such records exist. Do not invent sample-size thresholds or profitability conclusions.

**10. Keep the method stable**

Change facts, assumptions and selections as evidence changes. Change this standard only when an identified process defect, better evidence or an explicit user requirement warrants a general rule change.

Record each method revision here: version/date, prior rule, replacement, reason/evidence and affected steps. Apply revisions consistently across players and future games. Do not silently change standards in response to one outcome, a preferred player or criticism.

New session reports reference this file and its version. They may document game-specific assumptions, but must not introduce a parallel selection method.

Apply a revision prospectively. A retrospective review may test an old card against it, but must not relabel the original recommendation as having followed a method adopted later.

**Consistency example**

When an established outside receiver joins an offense, analyze his intended routes and scoring role, the work vacated by departures, other arrivals and the returning players. Then reassess his props, the other receivers, the receiving back, tight ends and quarterback markets together. His presence may divert targets, alter coverage or improve drive survival. Do not automatically dismiss his old-team history while accepting an incumbent's history from a materially different offense. Any selection must survive the same role, matchup and price comparison.
